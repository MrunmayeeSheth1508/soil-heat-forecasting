## 1. Introduction and Motivation

Underground power cables are the preferred transmission medium in urban areas, renewable energy collection systems, and grid reinforcement projects where overhead lines are constrained by land use or aesthetics. Their current-carrying capacity — **ampacity** — is governed not by the conductor alone but by the surrounding soil, which must conduct away heat generated by resistive losses. The governing equation, due to Neher and McGrath (1957) [Bates V, Ch 2], is:

$$I = \sqrt{\frac{T_c - T_a - \Delta T_d}{R_{dc}(1+Y_c) \cdot R_{ca}}}$$

where $T_a$ is the ambient soil temperature at burial depth and $R_{ca}$ contains the soil thermal resistivity $\rho$ as its dominant term — accounting for **70–90% of total thermal resistance** in 15 kV direct-buried installations [Bates V, Ch 1].

Both $T_a$ and $\rho$ are treated as **fixed design values** in current practice: a conservative (often worst-case) soil temperature and a single-point or assumed thermal resistivity are specified at design time and held constant for the cable's operational life. This conservative approach has two well-documented consequences:

1. **It produces large safety margins under normal conditions.** Bates (2021) measured that steady-state Neher-McGrath overestimates conductor temperature by a mean of **8% ± 8.9%** even when the design resistivity is known accurately [Bates V, Table 9.1]. In the Arizona PV case study in the same work, designed for conductor temperatures of 75–87°C, field measurement showed 55–61°C — an over-design of 15–30°C [Bates V, Ch 11].

2. **It prevents operators from using available headroom.** Dynamic Line Rating (DLR) for overhead conductors is established in practice and regulation. The underground equivalent — **Dynamic Cable Rating (DCR)** — remains operationally immature, in part because no method exists to supply continuously updated soil state (temperature and moisture) at cable burial depth.

Bracale et al. (2019) demonstrated the moisture → thermal resistivity → ampacity chain using a coupled soil-heat-and-moisture model driven by NWP, but validated it only model-against-model with no measured cable or soil data [Bracale V, survey]. Espedal et al. (2025) showed that an LSTM can tune lumped soil parameters against measured conductor temperature, but without any soil state as an explicit input, the improvement over physics-only baseline was marginal and scenario-dependent [Espedal V, survey]. Bates (2021) showed that USDA NRCS SCAN data (soil temperature and moisture at up to 40" depth) can replace the static design assumption for soil ambient conditions, but used a single in-situ measurement per season rather than a continuous forecast [Bates V, Ch 3, 11].

**The gap:** No published work supplies a *forecast* of multi-depth measured soil temperature and moisture — at the cable's burial depth, at hourly to 72-hour horizons — as a live input to a cable thermal model. Establishing this link is the contribution of this proposal.

## 2. Research Objectives

**Primary objective:** Develop and evaluate a pipeline that combines a data-driven multi-depth soil state forecaster (SoilSens, building on completed work) with an IEC 60287 / Neher-McGrath cable thermal model to produce dynamic cable ampacity ratings, and validate the cable-model component against a published multi-bedding field dataset.

**Secondary objectives:**

1. Quantify the forecast accuracy of soil temperature and moisture at cable burial depth (50–100 cm) for 24 h and 72 h horizons against a persistence baseline, using held-out USCRN station data.
2. Validate an IEC 60287-equivalent cable thermal model against the Darmstadt (Verschaffel-Drefke et al. 2021) field dataset, covering at least configurations IV (direct-buried, four beddings) and V/VI (duct installations).
3. Map the sensitivity of computed ampacity to soil temperature and moisture inputs, to bound the operational value of soil forecasting relative to the existing static-design conservatism documented by Bates (2021).

## 3. Background: Prior Work (SoilSens) — What Is Already Done

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The work described in this section is **[Completed]** and provides the foundation for the proposed ampacity extension. It is not claimed as new contribution in this proposal.

The SoilSens project established a pipeline for forecasting multi-depth soil temperature and moisture from USCRN observations:

- **Data:** USCRN hourly observations at 39 stations (2012–2021), 38 fields confirmed including soil temperature and moisture at 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 cm depth. No quality flags exist for soil temperature or moisture fields (confirmed from `uscrn_HEADERS.txt`).
- **Forecasting baselines scored:** persistence and physics-based baselines at 20 cm and 50 cm depth for 24–72 h horizons.
- **Architecture evaluated:** hybrid differentiable model (following Widyastuti et al. 2026) combining an LSTM for surface forcing with a Crank-Nicolson PDE solver for subsurface heat propagation; attention-LSTM for joint temperature/moisture forecasting (following Li et al. 2022).
- **Key finding:** Persistence is a strong baseline at short horizons; outperforming it at 24–72 h is achievable with physics-constrained architectures but requires careful treatment of moisture, which is the harder prediction target.
- **What is NOT done:** (a) No forecast has been run at 100 cm depth (needed for cable burial depth interpolation); (b) no moisture forecast has been scored against a baseline; (c) no cable thermal model has been connected.

## 4. Methodology

The proposed work has three components: **(A)** soil state forecasting at cable depth, **(B)** cable thermal modelling and validation, and **(C)** integration into a dynamic ampacity rating pipeline.

---

### 4.1 Component A — Soil State Forecasting at Cable Burial Depth

**Target depth:** A typical PG&E primary duct installation (4" PVC Schedule 40, 30" minimum cover) places the cable centre at approximately **0.82 m** below grade [EDS 038193/062288 V]. The nearest USCRN sensors are at 50 cm (above) and 100 cm (below), requiring depth interpolation. For the Darmstadt validation (Section 4.2), the environmental boundary conditions are taken directly from the Darmstadt experiment's own air and soil measurements — USCRN is not used for that component.

**Depth interpolation approach [Proposed]:**

The 1D heat conduction equation with depth-dependent diffusivity (used in Brunetti et al. 2022 and Widyastuti et al. 2026) provides a physics-consistent interpolation between measured layers:

$$\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\alpha(z) \frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\right)$$

With observed temperatures at 50 cm and 100 cm as Dirichlet boundary conditions and the Crank-Nicolson discretisation (unconditionally stable), the temperature at any intermediate depth — including 0.82 m — can be estimated directly. The diffusivity profile $\alpha(z)$ is either taken from the Brunetti et al. (2022) Bayesian inversion (applied to USCRN data [Proposed]) or set to a reasonable climatological prior from the Wilson et al. (2016) soil texture database (27 of 39 USCRN stations available).

**Forecast targets [Proposed]:**
- Soil temperature at 0.82 m: 24 h and 72 h horizons
- Soil moisture at 50 cm (as a proxy for burial-depth moisture): 24 h and 72 h horizons
- Evaluation against persistence (established as the hard-to-beat baseline by Bracale et al. 2019 [V])

**Moisture → thermal resistivity conversion [Advanced, following Bracale 2019 and Bates 2021]:**

The thermal resistivity $\rho(\theta)$ is related to volumetric moisture content $\theta$ through the Johansen relation (soil-type dependent). The key design values from the literature are:

| Condition | $\rho$ (°C·cm/W) |
|---|---|
| FTB at 3% MC | ≤ 60 (high-strength) — EDS 038193 [V] |
| FTB at 0% MC | ≤ 75 (high-strength) — EDS 038193 [V] |
| Boulder CO in-situ (wet) | 112–117 — Bates [V] |
| Typical industry design range | 50–300+ — Bates [V] |

The Johansen relation is parameterised by soil texture data from Wilson et al. (2016). Where texture data is unavailable (12 of 39 stations), sensitivity bounds will be reported rather than a point estimate.

### 4.2 Component B — Cable Thermal Model and Darmstadt Validation

**Thermal model [Proposed]:**

An IEC 60287-equivalent steady-state thermal model will be implemented, following Neher-McGrath (1957) as described in detail by Bates (2021) [V, Appendix A]:

$$R_{ca} = q_e \cdot T_i + T_j + T_d + \rho_e \cdot T_e$$

where the soil thermal resistance $T_e$ is evaluated using the image-source method (Brakelmann et al. 2021 [V, survey]) for multi-cable configurations. For transient analysis, the cyclic rating factor from IEC 60853 (paywalled — described in Bracale 2019 [V]) will be applied using the load factor approach validated by Bates (2021) to give mean error 0.3% [V, Ch 9].

**Cable parameters available:**

| Parameter | Source |
|---|---|
| MV150 Al: OD 34 mm, DC resistance 0.206 Ω/km at 20°C | Darmstadt paper [V] |
| MV150 Al: insulation 5.5 mm, sheath 2.5 mm | Darmstadt paper [V] |
| Darmstadt conductor temp limit: 90°C (MV) | Darmstadt paper [V] |
| Southwire 350 kcmil Cu: 0.031 Ω/1000ft at 25°C, 1.342" OD | Southwire SPEC 46319 [V] |
| Insulation thermal rho: EPR 350, PVC 500, fiber duct 480 °C·cm/W | N-M Table 6, Bates [V] |

Missing for the thermal model: thermal conductivity and density of each cable layer — these will be taken from Bracale et al. (2019) Table 2 values (insulation 3.5 mK/W, 2.00 MJ/m³K) as the only published measured values in the literature corpus [V, survey_bracale].

**Darmstadt installation geometry [V, corrected 4 Nov 2026]:**

Installation geometry follows Figure 2 of Verschaffel-Drefke et al. (2021). The main-field configurations occupy nominal burial levels of 0.65–0.85 m. The 0.65 m dimension in Figure 2 is interpreted as the **underside of the upper-row trefoil bundle** (figure-based interpretation; no unambiguous written definition exists in the paper). Under this interpretation and assuming touching 34 mm cables:

| Conductor location | Depth below surface |
|---|---|
| Two lower conductor centres | 0.633 m |
| Upper conductor centre | 0.604 m |
| Centroid of three centres | 0.623 m |

The 0.65–0.85 m range in the paper text refers to different configurations (main field vs duct and deep configurations), not uncertainty about a single trefoil. A depth-reference sensitivity study will be retained for model validation reporting.

**Bedding properties available from Darmstadt workbook [V, audit]:**

| Bedding | Thermal conductivity curve | Moisture retention curve | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sand | Yes (workbook TH sheet) | Yes | Yes (solid-only; correction needed) |
| Loam | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| FTB | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Silt | Yes | Yes | Yes |

The workbook's "volumetric heat capacity" column is solid-phase only (specific heat × grain density). It must be corrected: $C_{vol} = (1-n) \cdot C_{solid} + \theta \cdot C_{water}$ where $n$ is porosity [V, darmstadt_workbook_audit.md].

**Validation dataset (Darmstadt) — released configurations [V]:**

| Configuration | Duration | What is measured |
|---|---|---|
| MF_IV_CL | 39 days | Conductor temperature, 4 beddings, constant load |
| MF_IV_EVU | 5 days | Conductor temperature, EVU load profile |
| MF_IV_PV | 5 days | Conductor temperature, PV load profile |
| SF_V+VI | 51 days | Conductor + pipe temperature, PVC duct (V unfilled, VI grouted) |
| MF_III_CL | 35 days | Sheath temperature + moisture at cable surface |

MF_IV_CL at 39 days provides the primary steady-state validation. SF_V+VI provides validation for duct installations (directly relevant to PG&E PVC conduit geometry). MF_III_CL provides moisture-at-cable-surface dynamics — useful for the soil drying/rewetting analysis.

**Validation approach:**

Following Bates (2021) [V, Ch 9–10], the primary metric will be mean signed error and 90% CI for predicted vs measured conductor temperature. The target performance is ≤ 10% mean error (within the accuracy of CYMCAP/ETAP documented by Bates at -3% to -4% [V, Table 10.6]). Load factor correction following Bates [V, Ch 9] will be applied for the non-constant load profiles.

### 4.3 Component C — Integration: Forecast Soil State → Dynamic Ampacity

**The pipeline [Proposed]:**

```
USCRN hourly obs (T, moisture, 5/10/20/50/100 cm)
        ↓
  SoilSens forecast model (24h, 72h)
        ↓
  T_soil @ 0.82 m  +  moisture @ burial depth
        ↓
  Johansen relation → ρ(θ)
        ↓
  IEC 60287 cable model (validated in Component B)
        ↓
  Dynamic ampacity rating I_max(t+24h), I_max(t+72h)
        ↓
  Comparison vs static design ampacity
```

**Reference installation parameters:**

A PG&E-style single-circuit primary duct (4" PVC Sch 40, 30" cover, cable centre 0.82 m) with Southwire 350 kcmil Cu cable (Southwire SPEC 46319 [V]) and FTB backfill (EDS 038193 [V]) will be used as the reference. Static design ampacity uses Black Books assumptions (20°C ambient, RHO 90 °C·cm/W) [Bates V, Ch 2–3].

**Evaluation [Proposed]:**

The dynamic ampacity rating will be evaluated relative to:
1. The static design rating (to bound operational headroom)
2. A persistence-based rating (soil state held constant = today's value projected forward)
3. Climatological 99th-percentile conditions from USCRN (analogous to Bates's SCAN lookup [V, Ch 11])

The key claim will be: for what fraction of the year does the forecast-driven rating exceed the static rating by more than a threshold (e.g., 5%), and at what forecast horizon does the forecast beat persistence?

## 6. Expected Contributions

### 6.1 Novel (not in prior literature)
1. **Forecast soil state → dynamic cable rating** at 24 h and 72 h horizons, evaluated against a persistence baseline at a statistically meaningful number of sites (up to 39 USCRN stations), with soil temperature and moisture as explicit model inputs. No prior work does this [gap map, LITERATURE_MATRIX.md].
2. **Depth interpolation of USCRN observations** to cable burial depth using a physics-consistent 1D heat conduction model, enabling application to any station without additional sensors.

### 6.2 Advanced (building on prior art, cited)
3. **Moisture → resistivity → ampacity chain** as an operational pipeline — established in principle by Bracale (2019) [V] but not validated against measured cable temperature or run as a continuous forecast.
4. **N-M with dynamic load factor** applied to forecast conditions — method validated by Bates (2021) [V] for static inputs; here extended to forecast inputs.

### 6.3 Completed (SoilSens prior work, not claimed as new)
5. Multi-depth soil temperature and moisture forecasting from USCRN at 24–72 h horizons; persistence-baseline evaluation framework.

### 6.4 Claims explicitly NOT made
- "First" use of moisture in ampacity modelling — Bracale (2019) [V]
- Measured field ampacity at a USCRN station — not in scope
- Compliance with IEC 60287 or IEEE Std 835 — paywalled, not verified
- Soil drying unlikely in practice — this is Bates's conclusion for his specific site; the literature shows drying does occur (Darmstadt sand, Verschaffel-Drefke [V])
- Additional safety factor beyond N-M — Bates shows this is unwarranted [V, Ch 9]; claimed only within that context
